The operating model
"Discipline at the core, flexibility at the edge."

You own and certify the shared semantic models — the single source of truth. The business units build their own reports on top. Microsoft calls this managed self-service, and it's the answer when a central team can't hand-build everything.

It's the shape GoPlant is already taking — you own the model, consumers get reports. We're generalizing a pattern you've started, not inventing one.

The governance dial = ownership × delivery breadth × criticality. One analyst's report needs almost none; the sales commission model needs a lot. Don't govern everything to one rule.

Same principles, scaled ceremony

The architecture by department size

Small dept

2 workspaces (data + reporting). Promoted model. dev→prod (manual ok). App distribution.

Large dept

Multiple data/reporting workspaces. Certified models. dev/test/prod pipelines. Roles via security groups. Git on .pbip.

Enterprise

Fabric domains + delegated admin. Tenant-settings strategy. Capacity governance + chargeback. A CoE function.

Scale the ceremony to the stakes. The principles never change — separate model from report, least privilege, one source of truth — only how much machinery wraps them. And the dividing line between rungs is mostly licensing.

Organizing dozens-to-hundreds of workspaces (Fabric)

Domains — a management boundary per business area

  • Group all of Sales (or Finance, HR…) workspaces into one domain — a data-mesh: distributed ownership, central/federated governance.
  • Three-tier delegated admin: you (Fabric admin) appoint domain admins = the business owners, who self-manage their slice within your guardrails.
  • Organize by subject area, owning team, business unit, or project — and discover via the OneLake catalog.

Why it matters

It's how a 2-person team hands day-to-day control to business owners without losing central governance.

The operating function

You don't need a CoE to do CoE work

  • Microsoft: a chartered Center of Excellence is recommended, not required — a central data team commonly absorbs it. That's you.
  • Start centralized; evolve to federated (delegating to domain owners) as the estate grows.
  • Govern iteratively and lightly — the most lightweight model that meets the goal. Permission to not over-engineer.

Minimal cadence: weekly triage/onboarding · monthly usage + certification review · quarterly tenant-settings + maturity re-rating + capacity health. Plus a published naming convention and a data-ownership policy.

Microsoft — Center of Excellence · governance (iterative). Cadence is P3 judgment.

What to do now vs later

Adopt as you grow

Now · any size

  • Separate data vs reporting workspaces
  • Shared model + thin reports
  • Security-group roles, least privilege
  • Promoted endorsement + naming convention
  • .pbip + Git; a data-ownership policy

Later · as you scale

  • Fabric domains + delegated admin
  • Full dev/test/prod (start dev/prod)
  • Deployment pipelines (PPU/capacity)
  • Certified-endorsement program
  • Chargeback / showback

Framing derived from the maturity ladder — P3 judgment, not a verified practitioner survey.

The immediate job

Taking over the orphaned sales reports

  • Inventory & reassign — find everything the departed user owned; move dataset/gateway ownership to the team / a service principal, not another person.
  • Refactor & validate before taking ownership — reverse-engineer the hidden Power Query, RLS, and DAX; confirm the numbers.
  • Re-platform into separated workspaces; publish the model; convert to thin reports.
  • Endorse, document, assign an owner — then add a data-ownership policy so turnover can't orphan content again.

⚠️ Preserve the model interface. Renaming tables/columns/measures, changing data types, or moving a measure's home table breaks every dependent report. Refactor the inside; keep the surface stable.

Workspace creation

Not everyone, not fully centralized — a "Fabric Workspace Creators" security group of trained central + trusted local users.

Scope tenant settings to security groups, not the whole org — capable teams get more flexibility within your guardrails. Two governance levels only: governed / ungoverned. Keep it simple.

Where to start

Recommended sequence

  • 1 · Confirm the licensing tier (Pro / PPU / Fabric + consumer counts) — it gates everything.
  • 2 · Pilot managed self-service on Sales — separated workspaces, shared model, thin reports, an App, dev→prod.
  • 3 · Take over & stabilize the orphaned reports; assign owners; write the data-ownership policy.
  • 4 · Publish the standards (naming, roles, governance levels, "definition of certified") + the maturity ladder; rate each department.
  • 5 · Add scale machinery as justified — pipelines/Git at PPU/capacity; domains + chargeback at Fabric.
The one thing to remember

Own the core. Free the edge. Scale the ceremony to the stakes.

One operating model — managed self-service — applied at the right intensity for each department's size and criticality. Place every team on the ladder, give it the next rung, and let licensing tell you how far you can climb today.
