# Consulting Methodology

# The OUSDPS Framework

> **Observe → Understand → Simplify → Design → Prototype → Sustain**

Our methodology is designed to reduce risk, simplify complexity, and ensure every recommendation serves the business rather than the technology.

Each phase builds upon the previous one.

We do not skip steps.

---

# 1. Observe

> *Reality before assumptions.*

Before recommending solutions, we observe how work is actually performed.

Our objective is to understand the current state—not how processes are documented, but how they operate in practice.

Activities include:

- Site visits
- Job shadowing
- Process observation
- Reviewing existing documentation
- Identifying informal workarounds
- Capturing pain points

**Key Question**

> *What is actually happening?*

**Deliverables**

- Observation notes
- Initial process maps
- Pain point log
- Stakeholder list

---

# 2. Understand

> *Understand the problem before solving it.*

Observation tells us what is happening.

Understanding explains why.

During this phase we work closely with stakeholders to uncover business objectives, constraints, dependencies, and root causes.

Activities include:

- Stakeholder interviews
- Requirements gathering
- Root cause analysis
- Current-state process mapping
- Business objective definition
- Data and system review

**Key Question**

> *Why does this process exist?*

**Deliverables**

- Current-state process maps
- Business requirements
- Root cause findings
- Success criteria

---

# 3. Simplify

> *Remove unnecessary complexity before adding technology.*

Many operational problems disappear when unnecessary work is eliminated.

Technology should never automate waste.

Activities include:

- Eliminating redundant steps
- Standardizing workflows
- Clarifying ownership
- Removing duplicate data entry
- Challenging assumptions
- Identifying quick wins

**Key Question**

> *Can this be made simpler?*

**Deliverables**

- Future-state process maps
- Improvement opportunities
- Prioritized recommendations

---

# 4. Design

> *Design solutions that fit the business.*

Only after understanding the business do we begin designing solutions.

Technology is considered one option among many.

Activities include:

- Solution architecture
- Workflow design
- Technology evaluation
- Data flow design
- KPI definition
- Functional requirements
- Implementation roadmap

**Key Question**

> *What is the simplest solution that creates lasting value?*

**Deliverables**

- Future-state solution design
- Functional requirements
- Solution architecture
- Implementation roadmap

---

# 5. Prototype

> *Validate before investing.*

Large implementations are expensive.

Whenever practical, ideas should be tested before committing significant time or money.

Activities include:

- Python automations
- Dashboard prototypes
- AI proof-of-concepts
- Workflow simulations
- Pilot projects
- User feedback sessions

**Key Question**

> *Can we prove this works before scaling it?*

**Deliverables**

- Working prototype
- Pilot results
- Lessons learned
- Updated recommendations

---

# 6. Sustain

> *Ensure success beyond the project.*

The goal is not simply to deliver recommendations.

The goal is to leave the organization capable of successfully implementing, maintaining, and improving the solution.

Implementation may be completed by the client, internal teams, software vendors, or implementation partners.

Our role is to ensure the path forward is clear, practical, and aligned with the original business objectives.

Activities include:

- Final documentation
- Knowledge transfer
- Implementation guidance
- Vendor collaboration
- Success measurement
- Post-project review

**Key Question**

> *Can the organization successfully own this solution?*

**Deliverables**

- Implementation package
- Final documentation
- Knowledge transfer
- Success metrics
- Follow-up recommendations

---

# Guiding Principles

Throughout every engagement we follow these principles:

- Observe before recommending.
- Understand before designing.
- Simplify before automating.
- Prototype before investing.
- Build only what creates value.
- Leave clients more capable than when we arrived.

---

# Success

A successful engagement is one where:

- Complexity has been reduced.
- The business better understands its operations.
- Technology supports the process instead of driving it.
- Decisions are based on clear information.
- The client can confidently move forward without unnecessary dependence on us.

Success is measured by operational improvement—not by the amount of technology implemented.
